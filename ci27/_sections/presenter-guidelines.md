## Presenter guidelines {#presenter-guidelines}

### Talks

- **Timing:** standard talks last **10–12 minutes**, lightning talks **6–8 minutes**, both
  including questions. Plan for roughly one slide per minute and stay within your slot.
- **Slides and videos:** upload your slides well in advance and test any videos on the
  presentation system before your session.
- **Know your audience:** the room mixes computer scientists and Earth scientists, so assume
  as little background knowledge as possible.
- **Keep the maths light:** define every term and show only what is needed to understand your
  results.
- **Readable figures:** labels, titles and text should be large enough to read from the back
  of the room.
- **Little text:** keep text on slides to a minimum, so that the audience listens rather than
  reads.
- **Practise:** rehearse in front of a friendly audience to get feedback on delivery and timing.
- **Enjoy it:** the audience wants you to succeed.

### Posters

- **Format:** **A0 portrait** (841 × 1189 mm), to fit the poster boards.
- **The 3-second rule:** use a clear, bold title and one central take-away, so that someone
  walking past can grasp your main finding in about 3 seconds from 2 metres away.
- **White space:** aim for about **40% visual content** (figures, charts, maps) and leave open
  space to guide the eye.
- **Visual hierarchy:** put your most important result in the centre and use headings to lead
  readers from the introduction (top left) to the conclusions (bottom right).
- **Interdisciplinary clarity:** define acronyms and avoid niche jargon. Earth-science posters
  can follow the EGU poster guidelines (minimum font size **16 pt**); ML/CS posters can use an
  evidence-first layout, as recommended by the MIT Communication Lab, and avoid heavy maths.
- **Legibility:** text and figure legends should be readable from **1.5–2 metres**, with high
  contrast between text and background.
- **Elevator pitch:** prepare a **20-second** summary for quick visitors and a **3-minute**
  walk-through for interested experts.
- **QR code:** add a QR code linking to your full paper, code repository or supplementary
  videos.
